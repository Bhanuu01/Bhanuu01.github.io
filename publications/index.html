Conference paper

In-Domain Data Augmentation for Dysarthria Severity Classification

2024

Presented at IEEE SPCOM 2024. The paper studies in-domain augmentation strategies for dysarthric speech, focusing on improving severity classification in a low-resource setting where generic augmentation methods are often a poor fit.

This remains the clearest published example of my early research interests: signal processing first, then machine learning decisions built on top of that foundation.

Journal article · 2026 acceptance

Fusion of data augmentation for improved dysarthria severity classification

Apr 2026

Accepted for publication in the Journal of Signal Processing Systems. This work extends my interest in speech and signal processing under constrained data conditions, and it remains one of the clearest links between my academic foundation and later ML work.

The paper studies how data-augmentation choices affect dysarthria severity classification when labeled speech data is limited, with an emphasis on improving robustness rather than relying on generic augmentation recipes.

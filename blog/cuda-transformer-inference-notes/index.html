CUDA notes on transformer inference, memory bandwidth, and kernel fusion

Apr 28, 2026 · 6 min read

My strongest CUDA lesson so far is simple: if the profiler disagrees with your intuition, trust the profiler. Transformer inference invites assumptions about compute, but in practice the path can become bandwidth-bound much earlier than expected.

Why fused kernels mattered to me

Fusing projection and attention reduced intermediate writes and reads to HBM, which made the experiment a useful way to reason about memory movement instead of only looking at arithmetic throughput.

What the profiler changed

NSight made it much easier to spot the gap between “we reduced bandwidth” and “we improved total runtime.” The remaining scalar fp32 projection loops kept pointing to the next step: use Tensor Cores more effectively instead of treating bandwidth wins as the entire story.

Kernel fusion taught me something more useful than a single optimization trick: how to ask better performance questions in the first place.
